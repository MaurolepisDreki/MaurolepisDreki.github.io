<p>My name was <span class="dzura-text">Λиʏᴇδ эʏиKʌxʌя-Зʌϻẅ δwиBэπʌxσд</span> (styled A'viid ev'Khazar-Samuu du'Befazog in common Eorzean) &mdash; a name given me by my parrents, Khazar of Roggad, son of Daviid and Rrahar, and Samuu of Befazog, daughter of Raval and Vaia, and a perminent reminder of who I was and where I came from.&nbsp; Where I'm from (Rridajiia) a person's full name has meaning and changes over the course of their life.&nbsp; For example, I was given the name A'viid at birth &mdash; a name that will follow me into death &mdash; and, because I was born in the principality of Befazog (a feudal province of Rridajiia), I should rightly be known as dii'Befazog &mdash; the dii'- prefix indicating where I was born &mdash; or even du'Befazog &mdash; the du'- prefix meaning that I am a resident citizen of that place.&nbsp; Additionally, the prefixes ev'-, buk'-, and har'- are familial prefixes meaning "daughter of," "son of," and "wife of" respectivly which are followed by the father and mother's names, respectivly, or only the husband's name in the case of a bonding.&nbsp; And, lastly, I should mention the pefixes jo'- and kav'- which are titles of state &mdash; jo'- being a title approximately translating as "vassal of" or "sworn warrior of", a prefix followed by the name of the person they serve, and kav'- being a title with a meaing like "Lord of" or "Ruler of"<sup><span style="font-family: Arial, sans; font-size: 0.7em;">1</span></sup> and is followed by the name of the region that is their domain.</p>

<p>I understand that things are very different here in Eorzea &mdash; and even the rest of the world for that matter &mdash; especially in the fact that familial names are shared across both genders, between families, and also throughout generations, as if they were one great big clan living and dying in the city they were born in without significant distinction between individual members of that communtiy.&nbsp; The notion does not seem appealing to me at all; but I suppose that when one is in Eorzea, one must do as the Eorzeans do.</p>

<p>As I said, I was born in Befazog, Rridajiia, &mdash; a little-known nation in the Far East of the Source with a single port by which they do limited trade with other nations &mdash; as the first-born and only daughter of <span class="dzura-text">Kʌxʌя вwkиΔʌʏᴇδ-Γʌʜʌя jσиBσяʌʏᴇ δwиBэπʌxσд δᴇиЯσшʌδ</span> and <span class="dzura-text">Зʌϻẅ ʜʌяиKʌxʌя эʏиЯʌʏʌɪ-Yʌᴇʌ δwиBэπʌxσд</span>, nearly 20 years before the Seventh Umbral Calamity struck Eorzea and shook the world.&npsb; My father enlisted as a solider in service to Boravi &mdash; then the young heir-to-be of the pricipality of Befazog &mdash; as a young man making his way in the world; as an enlisted, he originally swore his sword to Boravi's father, Kharra, but quiclky found himself assigned to the duty of guarding the young prince.&nbsp; And, as much time as they spent together, they became fast friends &mdash; a fact that eventually resulted in my father becoming the Right Hand of Boravi as the highest military leader under Lord Boravi himself &mdash; a fact that eventually cost him his life.&nbsp; It was also how he met my mother.</p>

<p>Samuu, my mother, was the daugher of a local tradesman who specialized in textiles and was, herself, born and raised in Befazog herself.&nbsp; I understand that their bonding was a happy afair for all involved, although I sadly know very little of the event, and I will never have the oportunity to learn more.&nbsp; As a textile merchant, Raval was a man who could provide for his family well, even for his third and youngest daughter, Samuu.&nbsp; Nevertheless, he was thriled when the young decorated general, Khazar, took an interest in his daughter and asked for him to deliver her unto him,<sup><span style="font-family: Arial, sans; font-size: 0.7em;">2</span></sup> as is the custom in Rridajiia.&nbsp; And, as also is the custom in Rridajiia, Raval tested Khazar's resolve by having him perform feats to prove himself worthy as her husband &mdash; although, to hear Boravi tell it, the trial was greater on Raval who was beside himself with the wory that Khazar might fail his tests or become offended by their simplicity, so great was his interest in seeing his young daughter bonded neigh unto nobilty.&nbsp; I remember very little of Lord Boravi's ramblings concerning my parrents &mdash; I know he meant well, and now that I am older I can better appreciate his attempt to keep my parrents' memories alive, but I was too young then and only had eyes and ears for the arts that had won my father his prestige, and in restoring his honor for having fallen to a beast in defense of his Lord &mdash; a dishonorable death for such a decorated warrior in most people's eyes, and one that I felt obligated to defend.</p>

<p>As I recall, Khazar's first trial to win the right of husbandry with Samuu had something to do with slaying a powerful and elusive beast that resided at the top of a cliff where men could not walk and bringing back a bag of its feathers.&nbsp; But Khazar went futher, also per Rridajiian custom, deomonstraiting the depths of his love by also bringing back its meat and an unhatched egg, gifting the egg to Raval as a token of his generocity and hosting a celebratory feast featuring the beast's flesh.</p>

<p>And second trial, per custom, was harder still.&nbsp; To demonstrate his fortiude and resolve, Raval demanded that Khazar dwell, unarmed and in isolation, upon the top of the coldest mountain for three days.&nbsp; And, apperantly, Khazar made quite a show of the affair: he gathered witnesses throughout the community, and relinquished not only his sword to Boravi, his closest friend, but also the majority of his cloaths so that there could be no doubt that he was entierly disarmed before being escorted up the mountainside and having some of his soldiers stand guard to ensure that he stayed there for the alloted durration.&nbsp; He was up there for five days without food or water on the snow-covered peak before Raval and Boravi took a search party to check on him.&nbsp; They were surprised and releived to find him sitting in his small-cloaths in the middle of a circle where snow could not fall, such was the heat radiating from his body while he meditated &mdash; he told them that he had been exepcting them, and offered to pay for their meal when they returned to Befazog.&nbsp; It was this event, Boravi always said, that Raval overshowed his interst in the affair, for he was most concerned that in challenging Khazar in this way and forcing him to go further as proof of his love that he had killed his daughter's greatest hope for happiness in life.</p>

<p>The third and final trial was one far more traditional: to prove that he had the ability to support his bride-to-be, Raval demanded a titdy sum of money to be paid in exchange for Samuu.&nbsp; This trial, however, was not one that Khazar could outperform, the salary of a soldier being far less significant than that of a merchant, and so he did the next best thing: he drew his sword and turned to Samuu, who was present at the proceeding, and knelt down as he offered it to her saying "my life is yours &mdash; dispose of it as you will."&nbsp; I can only imagine the full vison of that encounter, but I do know that of all the stories Boravi told of them, this was the one that I hated the most then and do love the most now &mdash; I can think of nothing more romantic than a man offering his life to me, offering to live or die for the sake of my happiness, especially if it was a man that I loved and was inclined to offer the same to in return.</p>

<p>Needless to say, but Khazar and Samuu were bonded shortly thereafter in the traditional Rridajiian way, and I was born shortly thereafter &mdash; presumably as the result of their consumation rite.&nbsp; It was only unfotrunate that their happienss did not last longer than the 10 moons that it took to make me.</p>

<p>In the first year of my life, Samuu fell ill, even to the point that my father requested that I be placed in the care of one of Boravi's nurse maids so that I did not succumb as well.&nbsp; She died before my first summer.&nbsp; Khazar was distressed by the fact, but not more that Raval who blamed Khazar for the illness that took his daughter.&nbsp; Raval distanced himself from us after that; and I was fortunate that my father, Khazar, was forever a man of duty who, rather than falling into despair at the loss of his wife threw himself with renewed vigor into the care and upbringing of his daughter insomuch as his other duties allowed.&nbsp; As a result, I frequent found myself as a guest in the palace where Boravi's servants could look after me while he attended to his duties as Boravi's cheif general.&nbsp; I aways loved and admired my father, and still do to this day &mdash; it is because of him that I became the woman I am on this day.</p>

<p>My father's funeral was held three days before my fifth nameday &mdash; the timing could not be helped.&nbsp; I remember little of the event, although I have attended many such funerals since &mdash; I remember being alone with one of the maids from the palace holding my hand as we all watched my father burn on that funeral pyre, Boravi standing at the front and saying a few words to honor his memory.&nbsp; I remember that I did not entierly understand what was happened, or why my father was not there to embrace me on my nameday.&nbsp; And I clearly remember being alone in my father's house for the first time as life began to go on for the rest of the community while my world was consumed by chaos as I realized that my father, a hero of Befazog, was never comming home.</p>

<p>By autmn, rumors had began to fly among the children that my father had lost his honor death &mdash; What kind of a soldier lets themselves get felled by a common beast on a routine hunt?&nbsp; And worse, they looked down on me, his daughter, even going so far as to say that he had disgraced himself by producing a daughter rather then leaving behind a son who could carry on his legacy.&nbsp; I tolerated it because I did not know what else I could do, until, one day, some of the boys got it in their head to start throwing stones at me &mdash; in that moment the dam that had been keeping my emotions all bottled up inside broke, and the next thing I knew was being struck in the face by the boy's father as he threatend to beat me to death.</p>

<p>It was not until I awoke wrapped in bangages with my left eye swolen shut that I learned what happened.&nbsp; Boravi, my first and only visitor, told me that I had thrown myself upon the boy who had lead in my stoning and with untrained strikes proceeded to beat him senseless by my hands and carve his face with my claws, causing his father to rush out to defend him from me.&nbsp; In his own fit of rage, the man had gone too far, and was now awaiting Boravi's judgement, which he had postponed to see if I would pull through.&nbsp; Boravi, ever the wise and kind leader, took into consideration that I had not died and, in light of all the wrongs and injuries involved, placed upon him a wereglid for his part in the afair and a warning that if the incident were ever to be repeated, his life would be forefit.</p>

<p>Boravi caused that I should live in the palace after that, and all but adopted me as his own son.&nbsp; I would say that he adopted me as his own daughter, but I could never be convinced to behave like a daugter after that, and was repeatedly caught spying on the young men at the training grounds and attempting to emulate them to train myself to be a warrior like my father so that I could bring him the honor he deserved and would have had from a son.&nbsp; So Boravi, seeing my potential through my determination, and knowing that I could not be disuaded &mdash; much less aided by intervention &mdash; caused that I should be given private lessons in martial arts, and enabled me to join the boys in their drills three winters later.</p>
